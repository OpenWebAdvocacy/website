More than two and a half years after Apple was required to comply with the Digital Markets Act (DMA) on 7 March 2024, **not a single browser vendor has been able to upgrade its existing EU users from Apple’s WebKit engine to its own**. [Apple's technical and legal barriers continue to prevent meaningful browser competition](https://open-web-advocacy.org/blog/apples-browser-engine-ban-persists-even-under-the-dma/), allowing it to limit the capabilities of the web on iOS and keep developers and consumers locked into its proprietary App Store ecosystem. These barriers deny us, and our customers, the genuine browser choice and web app competition the DMA was designed to enable.

Today, Open Web Advocacy is joining forces with an extraordinary coalition of businesses, industry associations and civil society organisations from across Europe to publish an open letter to European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, calling on the Commission to immediately launch formal enforcement proceedings against Apple and compel it to comply with the DMA. **Together, the signatories represent an estimated 45,000–55,000 EU-established businesses and organisations**, spanning browsers, software, cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, AI, e-commerce, gaming, journalism, publishing, startups and investment, alongside advocates for consumer rights, digital rights, software freedom, economic growth, innovation and fair competition.

But we cannot stop here. Every signature counts, and we need your help to make this impossible for the European Commission to ignore. Delay is Apple's greatest ally. Every month that passes without enforcement is another month in which Apple can entrench its control over iOS and undermine the web as a competitive alternative to its proprietary App Store. If Apple can delay compliance for long enough, the opportunity for genuine competition may disappear before it ever has a chance to take hold.

We are not asking for new legislation or additional obligations, but for the Commission to enforce the DMA as written and without further delay compel Apple to comply. We believe the European Commission already has the legal authority, tools and information needed to compel Apple to comply fully with its obligations under the DMA with respect to the web. What's needed now is the political will to act decisively and see enforcement through.

The benefits would be enormous. Consumers would enjoy greater choice, lower prices, better privacy and security, and more innovative services. Businesses could reach customers directly, reduce costs, and compete without depending on proprietary app stores. By making applications work across operating systems, a thriving open web could also enable new mobile platforms to compete and strengthen Europe's digital sovereignty.

The future of the open web is worth fighting for. With the law on our side and immense support from all sectors, we have an extraordinary opportunity before us. All we need now is for effective enforcement. This is a once in a generation opportunity to fundamentally change the digital world for the better, unlocking extraordinary possibilities for innovation, competition and freedom.

To everyone who has already joined us, we would like to extend a massive thank you. If you haven't already signed, please add your support today, encourage your colleagues to sign, and ask your employer and any organisations you're involved with to join us.

**Every signature strengthens our collective voice. Together, we can make sure this extraordinary opportunity is not lost.**
